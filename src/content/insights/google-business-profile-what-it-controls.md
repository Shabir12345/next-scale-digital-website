For a local service business, the Google Business Profile does more for
visibility than the website does. It is free, it takes an afternoon to fill in
properly, and most of the ones we audit have been left at whatever state the
setup wizard produced two years ago.

Here is what each part of it does, ordered by how much it actually moves.

## Primary category: the single highest-impact field

The primary category is the strongest relevance signal in local search. It
largely decides which searches you are eligible to appear for at all.

Get it wrong and nothing else compensates. A shop listed as "Auto repair shop"
that mainly does tires is competing in the wrong candidate set on every tire
search in its area.

There is usually one obviously correct primary category, and the test is simple:
which single phrase would a customer use to describe what you mostly do? Not what
you also do, not what you would like to do more of. What you mostly do.

## Secondary categories: what else you are eligible for

Additional categories widen the set of searches you can appear in without
diluting the primary. Ten is the limit and most profiles use two.

Every genuine service line deserves one. A roadside business that does lockouts,
boosts, fuel delivery and winching should carry categories for them, because each
is a search someone performs. Leaving them off means being invisible for services
you actively provide.

The constraint is honesty rather than volume: only add a category for work you
genuinely do. But do not leave one off because your website does not mention it
yet. The website lags the business; the profile should not.

## Services: the list under the categories

Under each category you can list individual services with descriptions. This is
mostly ignored and it is one of the easier wins, because it lets you state in
plain language what you handle, using the words customers actually use.

Two rules. Do not list the same service four times with slightly different
wording, which reads as padding and clutters the profile. And do not describe a
service with a price or a promise you have not confirmed with the owner, because
the profile is published in the business's name and a wrong price there is a
real problem.

## Reviews: prominence, and the part you cannot shortcut

Review count, average rating and recency all feed local ranking, and they feed
the customer's decision even harder.

Recency matters more than most owners expect. Forty reviews collected in one push
two years ago is a weaker signal than a steady handful arriving every month.

Owner responses are worth writing. They are visible to the next reader, they
demonstrate the business is active, and answering a critical review well does
more for a wavering customer than the review itself does against you.

## Service area: trim on deliverability, never on ambition

For a business without a storefront, the service area defines where you are
eligible to appear. Stretching it across an entire region does not increase
reach; it can dilute the profile and cost you position in the places you actually
dominate.

Set it to what you genuinely reach in a reasonable time. Remove a city because
you cannot serve it well, never because it feels tidier. If the owner says they
serve somewhere, it stays.

## Photos: less about ranking, more about the click

Photo volume and freshness are a modest ranking factor and a substantial
conversion factor. The profile with twelve real photographs of the shop, the
team and finished work gets chosen over the one with a logo and a stock image.

Real photographs, taken on a phone, beat professional stock. The customer is
trying to work out whether you are a real business.

## The description: last, and least

The business description is the field owners want to write first and it is close
to the least important. It has limited ranking effect. Write it plainly, say
what you do and where, and move on.

## NAP consistency: the quiet ceiling

Name, address and phone number need to match across the profile, your website
and the directories Google cross-references. Mismatches make Google less
confident that these listings describe one business, and that uncertainty caps
what the profile can achieve regardless of how well the rest is filled in.

This is unglamorous work with no visible output, and it is frequently what is
holding a well-optimised profile back.

## What the profile does not control

It does not fix a website that takes six seconds to load. It does not make you
appear in areas where four established competitors sit physically closer to the
searcher. It does not rank you in the organic results below the map pack, which
are a different system with different inputs.

And it will not, on its own, get a business into a category it does not belong
in. The profile amplifies what is true about the business. It does not invent it.

## Where to start

If nobody has touched the profile since setup, work down in this order: primary
category, secondary categories, services, service area, photos, review flow, then
the description. The first three usually account for most of the movement, and
they can be done in one sitting.

Changes to categories and services can show up within two to four weeks, because
you are editing a listing Google already trusts rather than asking it to trust a
new page. That makes it the fastest-moving work available on a local account, and
the reason it comes before anything on the website.
